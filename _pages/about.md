I am an astrophysics PhD student at **Trinity College, University of Cambridge**, working in
the Cavendish Astrophysics Group as a Gates Cambridge Scholar.

My research is on **extreme-precision radial velocity** measurements — the technique that
detects planets by measuring the tiny wobble a planet induces in its host star. Finding an
Earth-like planet around a Sun-like star means measuring that wobble at the level of a few
centimetres per second, which is slow walking pace, inferred from starlight. At that
precision the instrument and the data reduction pipeline are as much a source of error as
the star itself, so my thesis is about finding and removing those systematics.

I work on **HARPS3**, the high-resolution cross-dispersed echelle spectrograph built for the
[Terra Hunting Experiment](https://www.terrahunting.org/) and installed on the Isaac Newton
Telescope in La Palma. I have been involved in its assembly, integration and verification,
and I lead the adaptation and validation of its data reduction pipeline.

Before Cambridge I completed a BSc and an MSc in physics at the **University of Lethbridge**,
in the Astronomical Instrumentation Group. There I built the cryogenic far-infrared grating
spectrometer used to post-disperse the output of a polarizing Fourier transform
spectrometer, and made what were the first measurements of blazed diffraction grating
efficiency at cryogenic temperatures in the far-infrared — work aimed at future space
observatories such as SPICA-SAFARI and PRIMA.

Alongside my degrees at Lethbridge I played five seasons in goal for the **Pronghorns**
women's hockey team, finishing with the Canada West career records for games, minutes and
saves by a goaltender.
